I'm building **Project Hive.AGI** — an open-source "human-perspective AGI"
where computers learn to understand humans (what they find beautiful,
important, anomalous) rather than humans adapting to computers.

## The company structure
- **Goldman Forge** — commercial AI delivery (currently: automated tourism videos)
- **Goldman Global Research Labs** — open-source research (the AGI thesis)
- Revenue from Forge funds Labs. Labs research makes Forge better.

## What's built so far (6 completed "circles")

1. **Foundation:** Seed Package system + dual-LLM engine (MiniMax M3 + DeepSeek V4) + IPFS P2P exchange + Obsidian wiki
2. **Safety:** PII blur (faces + plates, code-enforced, no bypass) + CLA + governance
3. **Brand:** Two-face identity (Forge commercial + Labs research)
4. **Video pipeline:** Tourism footage → short-form Reels (fetch → LLM tag → human judge → cut → finish)
5. **Selection intelligence:** Captures where human editors OVERRIDE the AI's clip selections — the "override delta" is the valuable data (not the AI's picks)
6. **Full video loop:** Stock footage → 80-second finished promo video with VO, music, subtitles, branding — M3 vision-audited

## The core thesis
The "beauty standard" data (human judgments about what's beautiful in video) IS human perspective. Over many cycles, the system accumulates cross-domain knowledge of "what humans find worth recording" — that's the path to human-perspective AGI.

## The provenance gate (critical design decision)
- Stock footage (professional content) → Forge commercial use only, BLOCKED from the research network
- Human-captured footage (glasses/phone) → eligible for the research network
- Human JUDGMENTS about stock → eligible (we call this "hybrid seed" — human taste is human perspective regardless of what it judged)
- The gate is code-enforced, not policy

## My questions for you

1. **Is the "small circles" strategy sound?** I finish one complete loop, review it, then run the next. Each overlaps the last and adds new territory. Is this the right approach, or should I parallelize?

2. **Is the provenance gate correct?** Stock pixels blocked from Labs, but human judgments about stock are eligible. Is "editorial taste on professional footage" genuine human-perspective data? Or am I fooling myself?

3. **The hybrid seed claim:** I say human judgments about stock ARE Labs-eligible because human taste IS human perspective, regardless of source. Is this defensible? Or is it a loophole that dilutes the thesis?

4. **The AGI bridge:** From "editor picks beautiful stock clips" to "distributed human-perspective AGI" — is there a credible path? What's the missing link? When does the system actually become "AGI" and not just "a video editing preference tracker"?

5. **Outside-the-box:** What am I not seeing? What radical approach would make this 10x better? What would you do differently?

6. **The business model:** Forge makes tourism videos. Labs builds an open-source AGI. Is the connection real (commercial funds research, research improves commercial), or forced? What's a better commercial application that generates MORE human-perspective data?

7. **The hardest question:** If you were a skeptical AI researcher reviewing this project, what's the ONE thing you'd attack first? Where's the weakest link in the entire thesis?

Please be brutally honest. I'd rather hear "this won't work because X" now than after 100 more circles.
