# Music & Voiceover Direction — Legends of China Warriors

> The two audio elements that turn a slideshow into a film.

---

## A. Music

### What we want

**Light Chinese-instrumental underscore** over a soft ambient bed. The reference video uses exactly this register — traditional timbres (guzheng, erhu, dizi) sitting on a modern, spacious pad. It reads as "China" instantly without sounding like a period drama or a tourist-board cliché.

### Reference cues (for the composer or library search)

| Cue | What it sounds like | Where in our film |
|---|---|---|
| **Guzheng + ambient pad** | Plucked zither over a sustained synth drone. Spacious, not busy. | Opening hook (Shot 1) — single note lets ring |
| **Erhu enters** | Two-stringed bowed fiddle, lyrical, slightly melancholic. | Beijing/Shots 2–3, under VO |
| **Swells on hero shots** | A slow crescendo — pad + light percussion — lifts into the Great Wall wide | Shot 3 (Wall), Shot 4 lead-in |
| **Drop to near-silence** | Everything pulls back except a faint pad. Room for the image. | Terracotta Warriors face reveal (Shot 4) |
| **Lighter return** | Guzheng returns, brighter, major-leaning. Relief. | Water towns / tea (Shots 5–6) |
| **Resolve chord** | A clean, confident landing. Not a fade. | CTA outro (Shot 8) |

### Pacing map (sync to picture)

```
0:00  ┌─ guzheng note (ring)
0:06  │  erhu enters
0:14  │  ── swell (Wall hero)
0:22  │  ── DROP (warriors face) ── near silence
0:32  │  guzheng returns, lighter
0:46  │
0:50  └─ resolve chord
0:55    end
```

### Sourcing

| Option | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **ECH library / composer** | Owned or one-time | If ECH has a relationship with a composer or a licensed track from past promos, reuse it. Consistent audio brand. |
| **Artlist / Epidemic Sound** | Subscription | Search: "china cinematic", "guzheng ambient", "erhu cinematic". These libraries clear for commercial use under subscription. **Check the license covers ads** — some tiers exclude paid placement. |
| **PremiumBeat / AudioJungle** | Per-track | One-time sync license. Search the same terms. Good if ECH wants to own the track outright. |
| **Commission** | $300–$1500 | A composer writes to picture. Best result, highest cost. Justifiable if this template runs across multiple ECH tour videos. |
| **AI generation (Suno/Udio)** | Low cost, **disclose** | Workable for a bed, but risks generic output and unclear commercial clearance. Not recommended for the hero track. |

### What to avoid

- **Loud percussion / drums** — kills the calm.
- **Western orchestral swells** — reads "movie trailer," not "trusted guide."
- **Stock "Asian" music with pentatonic kazoos** — tourist cliché. The reference avoids this; so must we.
- **Vocal tracks** — any lyrics fight the VO.

### Levels (final mix)

| Element | Target LUFS / placement |
|---|---|
| Voiceover | **-16 LUFS** (loud, clear, always on top) |
| Music bed | **-23 to -25 LUFS** (ducked under VO; comes up ~3dB in the warriors drop and the outro) |
| Mix overall | **-14 LUFS integrated** (YouTube/Instagram spec) |

**Music must duck under VO.** Use sidechain compression on the music bus keyed to the VO, or automate the dips manually at each VO entry. The reference does this — notice how the music gets out of the way when the narrator speaks.

## B. Voiceover

### What we want

A **calm, confident, trustworthy** Australian-English voice. Lower register. Measured tempo — never rushed, never breathless. The vocal equivalent of the guide who has done this route a hundred times and is quietly certain you're in good hands.

### Casting direction

| Attribute | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Accent** | Australian English (matches the target market — AU/NZ travellers). Warm, not broad. |
| **Gender** | Either. Pick the read that sounds most "trusted local expert," not "announcer." Female voices often read warmer for travel; male voices often read more authoritative. Both can work — audition both. |
| **Age range** | 35–55. Young enough to sound energetic about the trip; old enough to sound like they've been doing this for years. |
| **Tone** | Confident, unhurried, slightly intimate — as if telling a friend about a place they love. **Not** a hard sell. **Not** a documentary narrator. |
| **Tempo** | Slower than conversational. ~140–150 wpm. Let the images keep up. |

### Delivery notes per beat

| Shot | Direction |
|---|---|
| 1 Hook | Almost a whisper. "China doesn't reveal itself all at once..." — draw the listener in. |
| 2 Imperial | Warmer, grounded. Establishing authority. |
| 3 Great Wall | Slight lift. Pride. "the real one." |
| 4 Warriors | **Slow down.** This is the emotional peak. "every face different. every face waiting." — pause between the two lines. Let the music drop carry it. |
| 5 Water Towns | Relax the voice. Relief after the intensity. |
| 6 Tea | Sensory, appreciative. "you taste it." |
| 7 Shanghai | Energy picks up slightly — the modern beat. Still controlled. |
| 8 CTA | Confident, landing. "Your legend starts here." — firm, not loud. |

### Sourcing the VO

| Option | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **In-house / ECH staff** | Free | If a team member has the voice, record it. Most authentic. Needs a quiet room and a decent mic (even a phone mic in a closet works for a draft). |
| **Voice talent (Fiverr / Voices.com)** | $50–$400 | Australian-English travel VO is a common brief. Provide this doc + the script. Ask for two reads: warm and authoritative. |
| **AI TTS (ElevenLabs / Murf)** | Subscription | Quality is now high for EN-AU voices. **Cost-effective for iteration** — generate 5 takes, pick the best. Disclose if the final uses synthetic voice (platform policies and trust). |

### The full VO script (one take, ~42 spoken seconds)

> China doesn't reveal itself all at once.
> It unfolds — over twelve days, five cities, and five thousand years.
>
> It begins in Beijing — at Tiananmen Square, and the Forbidden City,
> where emperors ruled, and history still stands.
>
> You'll walk the Great Wall — not a postcard version,
> the real one. Stone by stone, two thousand years in the making.
>
> In Xi'an, you stand before the Terracotta Warriors.
> Eight thousand soldiers, carved one by one —
> every face different. Every face waiting.
>
> Then the pace softens — the gardens of Suzhou,
> the still water of Wuxi. China, catching its breath.
>
> In Hangzhou, you taste it — Dragon Well tea,
> poured where it's grown.
>
> And Shanghai — where a four-hundred-year-old garden
> meets a tomorrow that's already here.
>
> Twelve days. Flights, hotels, and the stories — all included.
> From fourteen ninety-nine. Your legend starts here.

**Word count:** ~165 words. At 140 wpm that's ~70s of speech; at 180 wpm ~55s. The script leaves ~13s of music-only breathing room across the 55s cut (hook intro, warrior drop, shot transitions) — so target the slower, ~160 wpm read. Do not rush to fit.

### Recording spec

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Format | WAV, 48 kHz, 24-bit |
| Mic | Large-diaphragm condenser, ~15cm, slight off-axis |
| Room | Dead as possible — closets, blankets, or a treated booth. No reflections. |
| Takes | At least 3 full reads. Mark preferred. |
| Delivery | One clean WAV per shot line **and** one full continuous read. The continuous read is for backup timing. |

## C. If ECH wants AI-generated VO (cost-saving path)

Given Hive.AGI's stack and the cost question, the pragmatic path:

1. **Draft with ElevenLabs** (or Murf) using an EN-AU preset voice. Generate the full read.
2. **Spot the weak lines** — AI VO struggles with the Warriors beat ("every face different. Every face waiting.") where the pause and weight matter. Re-generate those lines with manual pacing markup.
3. **Decide:** if the AI read is 80% there and budget is tight, ship it (disclosed). If the Warriors beat doesn't land, hire a human for those 4 lines and comp them in.

The voice **is** the brand here. A great image with a flat VO sells less than a decent image with a great VO. Don't under-invest in this one element.
