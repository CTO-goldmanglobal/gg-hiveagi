# Script — Landscape (16:9, ~55s, Desktop)

> **Use:** YouTube, website embed (desktop), Facebook
> **Resolution:** 1920×1080
> **VO:** warm Australian-English, calm, lower register (same take as vertical)
> **Music:** light guzheng/erhu underscore (same track as vertical)

The voiceover copy is **identical** to the vertical — same narration take serves both cuts. What changes is the framing: landscape lets the wides breathe in their native aspect, and the Shanghai/Bund contrast reads stronger wide.

---

## SHOT 1 — HOOK · 0:00–0:06 (6s)

**Visual:** Slow aerial push over the Great Wall following the ridge line. Landscape lets the wall lead the eye left-to-right.

**VO:**
> "China doesn't reveal itself all at once.
> It unfolds — over twelve days, five cities, and five thousand years."

**On-screen text (lower third):**
> LEGENDS OF CHINA · 12 DAYS

**Music:** single guzheng note, lets ring.

---

## SHOT 2 — BEIJING / IMPERIAL · 0:06–0:14 (8s)

**Visual:** Tiananmen Square wide with the crowds for scale, then a sweeping establishing shot of the Forbidden City rooftop line. The horizontal expanse is the point.

**VO:**
> "It begins in Beijing — at Tiananmen Square, and the Forbidden City,
> where emperors ruled, and history still stands."

**On-screen text:** *BEIJING · DAYS 1–3*

**Music:** erhu enters softly.

---

## SHOT 3 — GREAT WALL · 0:14–0:22 (8s)

**Visual:** Wide of the Wall marching over green mountains to the horizon. Hero shot — landscape is where this shot lives best.

**VO:**
> "You'll walk the Great Wall — not a postcard version,
> the real one. Stone by stone, two thousand years in the making."

**On-screen text:** *THE GREAT WALL*

**Music:** swell, hold the wide 2s after VO.

## SHOT 4 — XI'AN / WARRIORS (EMOTIONAL PEAK) · 0:22–0:32 (10s)

**Visual:** Wide of the pit — ranks of warriors receding into shadow. Slow lateral dolly. Then push in to a single warrior's face.

**VO:**
> "In Xi'an, you stand before the Terracotta Warriors.
> Eight thousand soldiers, carved one by one —
> every face different. Every face waiting."

**On-screen text:** *XI'AN · TERRACOTTA WARRIORS*

**Music:** **drops to near-silence** for the face reveal.

## SHOT 5 — WATER TOWNS · 0:32–0:40 (8s)

**Visual:** Lateral pan along a Suzhou canal with a stone arch bridge. Cut to Lingering Garden corridor — the lattice window framing reads beautifully wide.

**VO:**
> "Then the pace softens — the gardens of Suzhou,
> the still water of Wuxi. China, catching its breath."

**On-screen text:** *WUXI · SUZHOU*

**Music:** guzheng returns, lighter.

---

## SHOT 6 — HANGZHOU / TEA · 0:40–0:46 (6s)

**Visual:** Tea master pouring Longjing into a glass, steam catching side light. Cut to West Lake with willows and a lone boat.

**VO:**
> "In Hangzhou, you taste it — Dragon Well tea,
> poured where it's grown."

**On-screen text:** *HANGZHOU · WEST LAKE*

## SHOT 7 — SHANGHAI · 0:46–0:50 (4s)

**Visual:** Yu Garden moon gate in the foreground, the Pudong skyline rising behind it — the old-new contrast is the strongest wide shot in the film. **This is the landscape cut's signature frame.**

**VO:**
> "And Shanghai — where a four-hundred-year-old garden
> meets a tomorrow that's already here."

**On-screen text:** *SHANGHAI*

## Landscape-specific notes

- **No cropping from vertical.** Re-source or re-frame shots for 16:9. The Great Wall and Shanghai contrast shots are materially better wide.
- Title cards and CTAs sit **center-frame or lower-third** — the wider canvas lets you place text without crowding the subject.
- The Terracotta Warriors pit wide (Shot 4) is the one shot that benefits most from landscape — the lateral dolly across the ranks is impossible vertical.
- End card should include the **price + URL + phone** if ECH uses one — desktop viewers expect more detail than mobile.
