# Voiceover Direction — Legends of China Warriors

> Read when generating TTS, casting, or directing VO for the tour video.

## What we want

A **calm, confident, trustworthy** Australian-English voice. Lower register.
Measured tempo — never rushed, never breathless. The vocal equivalent of the
guide who has done this route a hundred times and is quietly certain you're
in good hands.

## Casting direction

| Attribute | Direction |
|:---|:---|
| Accent | Australian English (matches AU/NZ market). Warm, not broad. |
| Gender | Either. Pick the read that sounds most "trusted local expert." |
| Age | 35–55. Experienced, not elderly. Energetic but grounded. |
| Tone | Confident, unhurried, slightly intimate. Not a hard sell. Not documentary. |
| Tempo | ~140–160 wpm. Slower than conversational. Let images keep up. |

## Delivery notes per beat

| Shot | Direction |
|:---|:---|
| 1 Hook | Almost a whisper. "China doesn't reveal itself..." — draw the listener in. |
| 2 Imperial | Warmer, grounded. Establishing authority. |
| 3 Great Wall | Slight lift. Pride. "the real one." |
| 4 Warriors | **Slow down.** Emotional peak. Pause between "every face different" and "every face waiting." Let the music drop carry it. |
| 5 Water Towns | Relax the voice. Relief after intensity. |
| 6 Tea | Sensory, appreciative. "you taste it." |
| 7 Shanghai | Energy picks up slightly. Still controlled. |
| 8 CTA | Confident, landing. "Your legend starts here." — firm, not loud. |

## The full VO script (~165 words, ~50s at 160 wpm)

> China doesn't reveal itself all at once.
> It unfolds — over twelve days, five cities, and five thousand years.
>
> It begins in Beijing — at Tiananmen Square, and the Forbidden City,
> where emperors ruled, and history still stands.
>
> You'll walk the Great Wall — not a postcard version,
> the real one. Stone by stone, two thousand years in the making.
>
> In Xi'an, you stand before the Terracotta Warriors.
> Eight thousand soldiers, carved one by one —
> every face different. Every face waiting.
>
> Then the pace softens — the gardens of Suzhou,
> the still water of Wuxi. China, catching its breath.
>
> In Hangzhou, you taste it — Dragon Well tea,
> poured where it's grown.
>
> And Shanghai — where a four-hundred-year-old garden
> meets a tomorrow that's already here.
>
> Twelve days. Flights, hotels, and the stories — all included.
> From fourteen ninety-nine. Your legend starts here.

## Recording spec (if human-recorded)

| Spec | Value |
|:---|:---|
| Format | WAV, 48 kHz, 24-bit |
| Mic | Large-diaphragm condenser, ~15cm, slight off-axis |
| Room | Dead as possible — closet, blankets, or treated booth |
| Takes | At least 3 full reads. Mark preferred. |
| Delivery | One clean WAV per shot line + one continuous read |

## If using AI TTS

1. Generate full read with MiniMax TTS.
2. Spot weak lines — AI struggles with the Warriors beat ("every face different. Every face waiting.") where pause/weight matters. Re-generate with manual pacing.
3. If the Warriors beat doesn't land, hire a human for those 4 lines and comp them in.

**The voice IS the brand.** Don't under-invest in this one element.
